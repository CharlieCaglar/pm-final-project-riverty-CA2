Slide 5 · Strategy

Problem, value proposition and hypothesis

Problem statement hook

Consumers who cannot pay their balance in full are struggling to confidently create sustainable instalment plans digitally, causing avoidable agent contact and putting both recovery performance and Riverty’s self-service ambition at risk.

Value proposition

A clear digital instalment journey that helps consumers understand their situation, agree to a plan they can realistically keep, and reach an agent seamlessly when needed. This improves sustainable recovery and reduces avoidable agent contact.

22%of portal visitors start the instalment journey
46%of started journeys end in an agreement
19%of new agreements miss a payment within 60 days (22.5% for variable or shift income)
27.6%agreement rate for consumers with 3 or more open claims
Data-backed hypothesis

Adding an affordability check before the plan options will raise the share of new agreements with no missed payment within 60 days by 3 percentage points (81% to 84%), while agent contact within 7 days stays below 33.0%.

Slide 6 · Research

Competitive analysis, workaround and journey map

Competitive analysis / workaround

Persona: a consumer in financial difficulty who cannot pay the full balance at once and needs an instalment plan.

No external tools are supported by the research. The main workaround is calling an agent when the digital journey does not give enough clarity, and 31.0% of journey starters contact an agent within 7 days.

Evidence
  • N-02 chose the shortest plan because it looked right and missed the second payment.
  • N-04 preferred calling because a person could explain the cost.
  • N-08 shows agents have to ask again for information consumers already entered online.

Slide 7 · Blueprint

Roadmap, MoSCoW scope and PRD highlights

Prioritization / roadmap

Persona: a consumer in collections who cannot pay their outstanding balance in full.
Primary metric: % of new agreements with no missed payment within 60 days.
Guardrail: agent contact within 7 days.

I overrode the AI on B1 (multi-claim journey), raising its value from 3 to 4 because consumers with 3+ open claims have only a 27.6% agreement rate.

Now lane B2 · explain why income is asked B3 · affordability check B6 · payday-aligned dates Cut

B7, because it can push consumers toward higher monthly payments. B8 (chat assistant on every page), because there is no evidence it solves the affordability problem.

MoSCoW scope

Must

  • Simple form: monthly income and essential costs
  • Calculate an affordable monthly limit
  • Show only plans within the limit
  • Edit the numbers and recalculate
  • If no plan fits, offer agent support
  • Short explanation of why income is needed

Should

  • Input validation
  • Better explanation of how income is used
  • Confirmation the plan is within the limit
  • Save and resume
  • Funnel instrumentation

Could

  • Support for variable income
  • Itemised essential costs
  • Payment-date integration
  • Agent visibility of the result

Won’t (now)

Multi-claim plans, open-banking verification, credit scoring, chat assistant, agent tooling.

Safety guard

Never show a plan above the limit. Never present the result as financial advice.

Evals
  • 100% of displayed plans within the limit
  • Plan options reached within 2 minutes
  • 100% of no-fit cases offer agent support

Slide 8 · Validation

Experiment plan

Hypothesis

Adding an affordability check before the plan options raises the share of new agreements with no missed payment within 60 days by 3 percentage points (81% to 84%).

Control (A)

Today’s journey with the existing plan terms and no affordability signal.

Variant (B)

Control plus the B3 affordability check, one change only.

Primary metric 81% → 84% No missed payment within 60 days. Baseline 81%, MDE 3 points.
Guardrail 31.0% → 33.0% Agent contact within 7 days. Baseline 31.0%, investigate at 33.0%.
Sample and read date 2,518 / arm 50/50 split, 6 weeks of enrolment plus 60 days of follow-up, read at day 102, p < 0.05.
SHIPIf lift is at least 3 points and the guardrail holds.
ITERATEIf the lift is positive but below 3 points.
KILLIf there is no improvement.

Slide 10 · Story

Friction points, the aha moment and what comes next

Friction points and the aha moment

The hardest part was that my first idea of success was wrong. In M3 I measured success as the agreement rate, but my feature only shows plans the consumer can afford, so it could lower agreements on purpose.

The aha moment came in the experiment brief: the real goal is plans consumers keep, so I switched to the share of new agreements with no missed payment within 60 days.

A second friction point was that the prototype crashed on a single apostrophe in the code, which taught me to test a prototype before trusting it.

Key takeaways
  • Define success as an outcome the consumer benefits from, not a count of sign-ups.
  • A guardrail can be tripped by the feature itself, because sending consumers with no fitting plan to an agent may raise agent contact.
  • A 60-day metric needs a 60-day follow-up, so test length depends on the metric.
What I would do next
  • Run the A/B test.
  • Replace the sample 70% rule with a compliance-approved affordability rule.
  • Test plan ordering so the most comfortable plan appears first.
  • Later, add multi-claim support, where agreement is only 27.6%.
